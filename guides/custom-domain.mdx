By default, every InkLoom project is accessible at a subdomain of `inkloom.dev` (for example, `your-project.inkloom.dev`). On the Pro plan and above, you can replace this with your own branded domain — such as `docs.yourcompany.com` — so your documentation feels like a seamless part of your product's web presence. InkLoom handles SSL certificate provisioning automatically once your domain is connected.

<Note>
  Custom domains require the **Pro plan** or higher. If you are on the Free plan, upgrade to Pro in **Settings > Billing** before following the steps below. See the [pricing page](https://inkloom.io/pricing) for a full plan comparison.
</Note>

## Add a custom domain

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your domain settings">
    In the [InkLoom dashboard](https://app.inkloom.dev), select your project and navigate to **Settings > Domains**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your domain">
    Click **Add Domain** and type the full domain you want to use — for example, `docs.yourcompany.com`. Click **Save**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a CNAME record with your DNS provider">
    Log in to your DNS provider (such as Cloudflare, Route 53, Namecheap, or Google Domains) and add the following CNAME record:

    | Type | Name | Value |
    |---|---|---|
    | CNAME | `docs` | `your-project.inkloom.dev` |

    Replace `your-project` with your actual InkLoom project slug, which is shown on the **Settings > Domains** page.

    ```
    docs.yourcompany.com  CNAME  your-project.inkloom.dev
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for DNS propagation">
    DNS changes can take anywhere from a few minutes up to 48 hours to propagate globally, though most providers update within 15–30 minutes. You can check propagation status using a tool like [dnschecker.org](https://dnschecker.org).
  </Step>
  <Step title="SSL is provisioned automatically">
    Once InkLoom detects that your CNAME record is pointing correctly, it automatically requests and provisions an SSL certificate for your domain. No manual steps are required on your end.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Verifying your domain

After adding a domain, InkLoom displays its current status on the **Settings > Domains** page. There are three possible statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pending** | InkLoom is waiting to detect the correct CNAME record in DNS. |
| **Verified** | The CNAME record is confirmed. SSL provisioning is in progress. |
| **Active** | SSL is provisioned and your domain is fully live. |

If your domain stays in **Pending** for more than an hour, double-check that your CNAME record is saved correctly in your DNS provider's control panel and that there are no conflicting A records on the same hostname.

<Tip>
  Use your DNS provider's propagation check tool (most providers have one in their dashboard) to confirm the CNAME record is live before contacting support.
</Tip>

## Using a subdomain vs. root domain

InkLoom recommends using a **subdomain** such as `docs.yourcompany.com` for the simplest and most reliable setup. Subdomains map directly to a CNAME record, which is straightforward to configure with any DNS provider.

Pointing a **root domain** (also called an apex domain, e.g. `yourcompany.com`) to InkLoom is more complex because most DNS providers do not support CNAME records at the apex. If you need to use a root domain, check whether your DNS provider supports ALIAS or ANAME records, which some providers offer as a CNAME-equivalent for apex domains.

<Warning>
  Avoid placing InkLoom behind a reverse proxy or CDN that rewrites hostnames (such as a Cloudflare proxy with orange-cloud enabled) without first consulting InkLoom's [SSL documentation](https://docs.inkloom.io/ssl). Proxying incorrectly can interfere with SSL certificate provisioning.
</Warning>

## Removing a domain

To disconnect a custom domain from your project:

1. Go to **Settings > Domains** in your project dashboard.
2. Find the domain you want to remove and click **Remove** next to it.
3. Confirm the removal in the dialog that appears.

After removing a domain, your documentation reverts to its `*.inkloom.dev` subdomain. Remember to delete the CNAME record from your DNS provider as well to avoid any dangling DNS entries.
